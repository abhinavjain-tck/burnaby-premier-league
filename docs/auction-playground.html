Rule 1

Money

In plain words
One wallet, or a wallet plus a locked one. The locked wallet opens only when your team is short of a must-have (Rule 3). Never for a Star. It works for one band only: the first time you use it picks the band. Leftover locked money is lost.
Example
  1. Kings: 40 cr left, 50 cr locked, short in the 5 cr band.
  2. A 5 cr band bowler comes up. Locked wallet opens. They win at 55 cr: 40 from the wallet, 15 from locked. Locked wallet is now tied to the 5 cr band.
  3. Next up, a 2 cr band player they also need. Locked wallet stays shut: wrong band.

Rule 2

Starting prices

In plain words
Organisers sort players into bands on Saturday, like seeding. The band sets where bidding starts. Higher starts for stars means fewer shouts to reach their real price.

Rule 3

Limits

In plain words
"At most" stops one owner buying every star. "At least" makes sure every team can field a side, and is what opens the locked wallet. The screen greys out a team that can't bid.

Rule 4

Bid steps

In plain words
Each shout raises the price by a fixed step. A ladder means small steps for cheap players, big steps for expensive ones.

Rule 5

Order

In plain words
Stars first sets the market early. A lucky dip is fair, but a star can come up when wallets are empty.

Rule 6

Unsold players

In plain words
Nobody is left out. Unsold players come back at the end. Still unsold? The organisers place them, starting with the team that has the most money left.
Try it: five unsold players
Rule 7

Price ceiling

In plain words
Should one player have a maximum price? There is already a brake: a team can never bid so much that it can't fill its squad at the cheapest price. A second brake: a team can only go past 11 if there are spare players in the pool once everyone can reach 11.

Rule 8

Countdown

In plain words
"Going once" starts a 10-second countdown on every phone. Does the sale happen by itself at zero, or when the operator taps SOLD? Auto is faster. Manual avoids "but I shouted!" arguments.
